I think one of the automatons that I found online while researching about gears that I found inspiring is the 'Antikythera Mechanism'. It's an analogue computer.

It's such a fascinating device because it could predict eclipses, track astronomical movements like those of the sun and moon, made up of just gears.

From [Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/ancientgreece/comments/1vkb1w4/the_antikythera_mechanism_is_absolutely_insane/):

> For example, ancient astronomers knew that Jupiter appeared to return to the same position in the sky 65 times every 71 years. This may seem like a weird set of numbers, but they always wanted to work with integers because, in antiquity, they worked in fractions as opposed to decimals. <mark>And, even more important for this device, that ratio of 65/71 can be translated directly into the numbers of teeth on the gears so that, for every 71 rotations of a gear associated with the sun, you get 65 rotations of the gear associated with Jupiter</mark>.

I find it interesting that automatons and clockwork have such a correlation. From class, we learnt that automatons are a mechanical object that mimics life in some way. I suppose for the 'Antikythera Mechanism', it's not life per say, but for the Greeks, there was a correlation between the cosmos and divinity, as a manner of bringing order to a living system. And so, maybe, it's defined as an automaton for mimicking the rhythms of the cosmos.
